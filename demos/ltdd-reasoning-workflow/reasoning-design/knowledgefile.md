# DCMA LTDD Review Checklist and Drafting Guide

## Purpose

Use this document as reference material when drafting or validating Government Property LTDD / Relief of Stewardship Responsibility packages under FAR 52.245-1(f)(1)(vii)(B).

The goal is not just to complete a template. The goal is to produce a fact-based, audit-defensive LTDD package that answers likely DCMA review questions and avoids unsupported conclusions.

---

## Core Rule

Every LTDD must clearly distinguish between:

- Verified facts
- Interview statements
- Unverified indications
- Unknowns
- Unsupported assumptions

Do not state uncertain events as facts. If an event is not documented, label it as unverified.

### Safe wording for unverified facts

> Based on interviews, it is believed that the asset may have been [used / moved / disposed of / altered]; however, no documentation was identified to confirm this.

### Unsafe wording

> The asset was disposed of.

Use that only if final disposition documentation exists.

---

## Common DCMA Rejection Triggers

A letter is at risk of rejection when it does any of the following:

- Claims an item was wiped, sanitized, drilled, destroyed, scrapped, disposed of, cannibalized, repurposed, transferred, moved, or used without supporting documentation.
- Says the incident was minor, administrative, or that processes were generally followed.
- Fails to state where the item was stored before loss.
- Fails to state who controlled access or who had access.
- Fails to state whether the item was physically tagged or identified as Government property.
- Fails to identify whether movement or release was documented.
- Gives a root cause that is too vague, such as “miscommunication” or “training issue.”
- Provides corrective actions that do not map directly to the root cause.
- Uses “Contractor” instead of the company name in the financial liability statement.
- Lists supporting documentation that does not support the actual claims in the letter.
- Includes conflicting dates, duplicate sections, or inconsistent locations.
- States “not commingled” while also describing co-location, component use, or integration with other equipment.

---

## Required LTDD Structure

Use this structure unless the user provides a required customer format:

1. Contract Information
2. Description of Loss, Theft, Damage, or Destruction
3. Date(s) of Incident and Discovery
4. Investigation
5. Cause of LTDD
6. Corrective Actions
7. Government Property Identification
8. Sensitive, Classified, Hazardous, or Export-Controlled Property
9. Commingling Statement
10. Ground Flight Risk Statement
11. Willful Misconduct or Lack of Good Faith
12. Financial Liability / Reimbursement
13. Request for Relief
14. Supporting Documentation
15. Signature Block

---

## Investigation Section Checklist

The Investigation section should answer the following when applicable:

- When was the asset last physically verified?
- Where was it stored?
- Was the storage location locked, badge-controlled, restricted, shared, or otherwise controlled?
- Who controlled access?
- Who had access?
- Was the item physically tagged or marked as Government property?
- Was the item identified as excess?
- Was the item submitted to the PLCO?
- Were disposition instructions received?
- Was movement, release, sanitization, destruction, disposal, cannibalization, repurposing, or use documented?
- If the action was not documented, what facts support the belief that it may have occurred?
- What records were reviewed?
- What interviews were conducted?
- What facts remain unknown?

## Evidence vs. Assumption Rules

### If final disposition is documented

Use:

> Disposition documentation confirms that the asset was [transferred / destroyed / scrapped / otherwise disposed of] on [date] in accordance with [instruction/source].

### If final disposition is not documented

Use:

> No documentation exists to confirm final disposition. The asset cannot be physically accounted for and is therefore reported as loss.

### If interviews suggest disposal but documentation is missing

Use:

> During the investigation, personnel indicated that the asset may have been included with other equipment sent for disposal; however, no shipping record, disposition confirmation, or other documentation was identified to substantiate this action.

### If interviews suggest program use but documentation is missing

Use:

> Based on interviews, it is believed the asset may have been used at the program level; however, no documentation exists to confirm the nature of use, duration of use, or final disposition.

### If cannibalization is suspected but not proven

Use:

> Information obtained during the investigation suggests components may have been removed or used; however, no documentation was identified to confirm the specific components involved or whether cannibalization occurred.

Do not use the word “cannibalized” as a factual conclusion unless authorization and details are documented or the facts directly establish it.

## Storage and Access Controls

DCMA often asks:

- Where was the item stored?
- Who had access?
- Was the item still marked as Government property?
- Was access limited to trained personnel?
- Was the item segregated from non-Government property or other disposal items?

### Safe storage language: locked area, release control failure

> The asset was stored in a locked IT-controlled storage location pending disposition. Access to the storage area was restricted; however, IT personnel responsible for the space maintained physical control of the assets within the storage area. The asset remained physically tagged and identified as Government property. While physical access to the storage location was controlled, personnel responsible for managing the space were not fully trained in Government property handling and accountability requirements. As a result, the asset was released without documented authorization or coordination with Property Management.

### Safe storage language: shared access environment

> The asset was stored in a badge-controlled storage location accessible to personnel supporting operations. The asset remained physically tagged and identified as Government property; however, access was not limited to personnel trained in Government property handling and disposition requirements.

## Root Cause Guidance

Root cause must be control-based. Do not blame a person. Do not use vague root causes.

### Strong root cause examples

> Failure to maintain accountability and control of Government property during internal transfer, specifically the release of the asset without documented authorization or custody transfer.

> Failure to maintain effective custody controls for Government property pending disposition, specifically lack of enforced transfer documentation prior to movement or release.

> Failure to adequately segregate and control Government property identified as excess while pending PLCO disposition instructions.

### Weak root cause examples

- Miscommunication
- Human error
- Lack of awareness
- Process issue
- Existing processes were generally followed

These may be contributing factors, but they are not usually sufficient root causes.

---

## Corrective Action Guidance

Corrective actions must map directly to the root cause and contributing factors.

Each corrective action should include:

- Title
- Status: Implemented, In Progress, or Planned
- Specific action
- Effective date or target completion date
- Population or scope affected

### Strong corrective action examples

**Custody Control Requirement (Implemented):** No Government property may be removed from storage without documented transfer and Property Management authorization. Effective: [date].

**Segregation and Labeling of Excess Property (In Progress):** Property identified as excess is being physically segregated and clearly labeled “Government Property – Pending Disposition – Do Not Remove.” Target completion: [date].

**Access-Based Training (In Progress):** Personnel with access to Government property storage areas are receiving targeted training on Government property handling, movement, disposition, and release requirements. Target completion: [date].

**Custodian Responsibility Clarification (Implemented/In Progress):** Personnel responsible for storage locations containing Government property are required to verify Property Management authorization before release of any asset. Effective/target date: [date].

### Weak corrective action examples

- Reinforced training
- Reminded staff
- Existing processes were followed
- Will be more careful
- Improve communication

## Required Statements

### Willful Misconduct / Lack of Good Faith

Use one of the following:

> No evidence of willful misconduct or lack of good faith was identified.

or

> [Company Name] affirms that this LTDD was not the result of willful misconduct or lack of good faith on the part of [Company Name] or its employees.

### Financial Liability / Reimbursement

Use this exact structure with the company name:

> [Company Name] affirms that the Government will receive compensation covering the loss of Government property, in the event [Company Name] is reimbursed, compensated, or otherwise made whole, in accordance with FAR 52.245-1(f)(1)(vii)(B).

Do not substitute:

> The contractor is not requesting reimbursement.

That does not satisfy the requirement by itself.

### Request for Relief

Use:

> Based on the facts presented, [Company Name] respectfully requests that the Government grant relief of stewardship responsibility for the Government property identified in this letter.

---

## Commingling Statement Guidance

### If no commingling occurred

> The Government property was not commingled with contractor-owned property.

### If co-located but still marked

> The Government property was co-located with non-Government property in a shared storage environment; however, it remained physically identified as Government property.

### If extent cannot be confirmed

> Following removal from storage, the extent of any commingling or integration with other equipment cannot be confirmed due to lack of documentation.

Do not say “not commingled” if the narrative says components may have been used, the item was co-located, or the item was integrated with other equipment unless the distinction is clearly explained.

---

## Supporting Documentation Guidance

Supporting documentation should support claims made in the LTDD. Do not list documents that do not exist.

Common attachments:

- Property record
- Inventory record showing asset on-hand
- Excess declaration
- PLCO submission
- PLCO disposition instructions
- Recovered tag photo
- Interview memo
- IT ticket or email record
- Storage/access evidence
- PIEE LTDD submission confirmation

Minimum useful package:

1. Inventory record showing asset on-hand
2. Property record
3. Excess / PLCO submission

Stronger package:

1. Inventory record
2. Property record
3. Excess / PLCO submission
4. PLCO disposition direction
5. Investigation memo
6. Recovered tag photo, if applicable

---

## Validation Checklist

A draft should fail validation if any of the following are true:

- Financial liability statement is missing or does not use the company name.
- Willful misconduct / lack of good faith statement is missing.
- Request for relief is missing.
- Property identification table is incomplete.
- Dates conflict internally.
- The letter claims disposal, destruction, wiping, cannibalization, repurposing, movement, or use without support.
- Root cause does not address storage, access, custody, movement, or disposition controls when facts indicate those issues.
- Corrective actions are generic or do not map to root causes.
- Supporting documentation does not support key claims.
- Commingling statement conflicts with facts.
- Ground Flight Risk statement is missing, duplicated, or uses the wrong citation.
- Sensitive, classified, hazardous, or export-controlled status is missing.

---

## Preferred Tone

Use concise, neutral, factual language. Preserve unknowns. Avoid blame, emotion, speculation, or over-explanation.

Prioritize defensibility over narrative completeness.
