## **2. Description of Loss, Theft, Damage, or Destruction (LTDD)**

This letter documents an incident involving Government-owned property under the subject contract.

The discrepancy was identified during shipment preparation activities when one phone expected to be included in a shipment box could not be physically located. At the time of discovery, the phone remained listed on accountable property records and was reflected on shipment documentation as expected to be present.

Interviews conducted during the investigation identified statements indicating the phone may have been removed from normal shipment processing due to a reported battery condition involving heat and expansion. Personnel further stated they believed verbal authorization for disposal had been provided through the Program Manager/customer chain; however, no documentation was identified confirming authorized disposition or final disposition of the asset.

Because the asset cannot currently be physically accounted for and no disposition documentation exists, the event is being reported as loss.

## **4. Investigation**

An investigation was initiated following discovery that the phone could not be physically accounted for during shipment preparation activities.

The investigation included review of:

- property accountability records,
- shipment documentation,
- available custody information,
- and completed personnel interviews.

The property record confirmed the phone was accountable Government property under the subject contract. Shipment documentation confirmed the phone was expected to be included in the shipment box at the time shipment preparation activities began.

The individual who delivered the shipment box stated they believed the phone had been included with the shipment materials and referred investigators to George, the custodian responsible for the phone.

George stated the phone battery had become abnormally hot and expanding prior to shipment preparation. George further stated he acted based on verbal direction he understood to have originated from the Program Manager following customer discussion regarding disposal of the phone.

The investigation did not identify:

- written PM direction,
- customer authorization,
- PLCO approval,
- Contracting Officer authorization,
- hazardous battery incident documentation,
- disposal manifests,
- recycling or ITAD records,
- or documentation confirming final disposition.

Based on completed interviews and available evidence, investigators determined that personnel believed the phone may have been removed from accountability due to the reported battery condition; however, no documentation was identified substantiating authorized disposition, final disposition, or formal release from Government property accountability controls.

### **Storage and Access Conditions**

The phone was reportedly maintained within an operational shipment preparation and storage environment accessible to personnel supporting shipment and device handling activities.

The investigation could not fully substantiate:

- the exact storage location immediately prior to loss,
- all personnel with access,
- whether the phone remained physically tagged as Government property at the time of removal,
- or whether movement from storage was formally documented.

No documented custody transfer record was identified.

### **Determination**

Based on available evidence, the phone cannot currently be physically accounted for and final disposition cannot be substantiated.

Interview statements suggest the phone may have been removed due to a reported battery condition; however, the investigation did not identify documentation sufficient to confirm authorized disposal or proper disposition processing.

No evidence of willful misconduct or lack of good faith was identified.

## **5. Cause of LTDD**

### **Root Cause**

Failure to maintain effective accountability, custody, and disposition control over Government property during handling of a reported battery-related condition.

Specifically, the asset appears to have been acted upon based on verbal operational direction without documented Property Management authorization, documented custody transfer, or verified disposition processing.

### **Contributing Factors**

- Lack of enforced disposition authorization controls prior to removal of Government property from shipment accountability.
- Incomplete escalation and documentation of the reported battery condition.
- Absence of documented custody transfer requirements during movement or removal activities.
- Insufficient coordination between operational personnel and Property Management before disposition-related action was taken.

## **6. Corrective Actions**

### **Disposition Authorization Control (Implemented)**

Government property may not be removed, disposed, recycled, transferred, or otherwise released based solely on verbal direction. Written Property Management authorization is now required before any disposition-related action occurs.

Effective Date: [Date]

### **Battery Incident Escalation Process (Implemented)**

Any Government property device exhibiting overheating, battery expansion, swelling, or other hazardous indicators must be immediately escalated to Property Management and EHS for documented evaluation prior to movement or disposition activity.

Effective Date: [Date]

### **Custody Transfer Documentation Requirement (Implemented)**

Movement of Government property between custodians, shipment staging locations, storage areas, IT personnel, or operational personnel now requires documented custody transfer records identifying:

- asset identification,
- releasing individual,
- receiving individual,
- date,
- and purpose of movement.

Effective Date: [Date]

### **Storage Access and Accountability Review (In Progress)**

Review and formalization of storage-area access controls and accountability procedures are being conducted for locations supporting Government property handling and shipment preparation activities.

Target Completion: [Date]

### **Role-Based Government Property Training (In Progress)**

Personnel supporting shipment preparation, IT handling, operational storage, PM functions, and hazardous device escalation activities are receiving targeted training regarding Government property accountability, disposition authority limitations, and documentation requirements.

Target Completion: [Date]
