## **1. Person Who Dropped Off the Box**

### **Questions That Establish Facts**

When did you last physically see the phone?

Where was the phone located at that time?

Who gave you the box for shipment preparation?

Did you personally verify the contents of the box before dropping it off?

Was the phone listed on the shipment paperwork when you handled the box?

Did you observe the phone being removed, transferred, or separated from the shipment?

Who else had access to the phones before shipment prep?

### **Questions That Test Assumptions**

When you said, “I thought it was in the box,” what made you believe that?

Were you assuming the phone had already been processed by someone else?

Did anyone tell you the phone had been disposed of, or did you personally observe that?

Did you see any documentation related to disposal or battery handling?

## **2. George, the Custodian**

### **Questions That Establish Facts**

When did you last physically see the phone?

Where was the phone stored at that time?

Was the phone still tagged or identified as Government property?

What specifically did you observe about the battery condition?

Did you create or receive any ticket, email, or incident report related to the battery?

What action did you personally take with the phone?

Did you move the phone anywhere? If so, where?

Who else was present or involved?

Was any movement or release documented?

### **Questions That Test Assumptions**

When you stated the PM approved disposal, did you personally see written authorization?

Did you personally speak with the customer?

Were you told disposal was approved, or did you infer that from the conversation?

Did you assume battery safety concerns allowed immediate disposal?

Did anyone from Property Management approve removal or disposition directly to you?

## **3. PM (Program Manager)**

### **Questions That Establish Facts**

Did you provide direction regarding the phone?

If so, what specifically did you instruct personnel to do?

Did you communicate with the customer regarding the battery issue?

Do emails, messages, or meeting notes exist documenting those discussions?

Was Property Management involved before any action was taken?

Was disposition authority formally requested or granted?

### **Questions That Test Assumptions**

When you discussed the battery issue, did you intend to authorize disposal?

Did personnel potentially misunderstand your guidance?

Did you assume EHS or IT had already approved handling or disposal?

Did you believe customer awareness automatically constituted disposal approval?

## **4. Anyone Involved in Disposal Approval or Battery Handling**

### **Questions That Establish Facts**

Did you physically handle or inspect the phone?

Was the battery condition documented?

Did any hazardous-material or EHS procedure apply?

Was any disposal, recycling, or transfer record created?

Was the phone sent to ITAD, recycling, scrap, or hazardous waste processing?

Do any manifests, tickets, or certificates exist?

Who had custody of the phone immediately before it became unaccounted for?

### **Questions That Test Assumptions**

Did you believe verbal approval was sufficient authorization?

Did anyone assume another group had already documented disposition?

Was the phone treated as hazardous material without formal disposition approval?

Did anyone assume the phone was no longer Government property because it was damaged?

## **Interview Goal**

The purpose of these interviews is not to prove a disposal story.

The goal is to establish:

- the last verified physical custody,
- whether documentation exists,
- whether authorization actually occurred,
- and where assumptions may have replaced accountable process.
