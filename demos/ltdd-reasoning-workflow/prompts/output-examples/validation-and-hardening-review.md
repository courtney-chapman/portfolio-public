## **C. Unsupported Assumption Risks**

The draft appropriately avoids stating disposal occurred as fact; however, several statements still create potential inference risk.

### **Potentially Risky Language**

“the asset appears to have been acted upon based on verbal operational direction”

Risk:  
This implies action occurred even though final disposition is unverified.

Safer alternative:

“interviews indicated personnel may have acted based on verbal operational direction.”

---

“removed from accountability”

Risk:  
Could imply formal accountability removal occurred.

Safer alternative:

“removed from expected shipment accountability location.”

---

“following removal from shipment accountability”

Risk:  
Implies confirmed movement/removal.

Safer alternative:

“following the period during which the asset became unaccounted for.”

## **D. Timeline / Consistency Issues**

### **Last Physical Verification Gap**

The package repeatedly references uncertainty regarding timing, but does not clearly establish:

- last confirmed inventory,
- last confirmed sighting,
- or last accountable custodian.

DCMA will likely ask:

“When was the item last physically verified?”

### **Storage Timeline Ambiguity**

The package states the phone existed within an “operational shipment preparation and storage environment,” but does not clearly establish:

- whether storage was temporary or permanent,
- whether shipment prep had already started,
- or whether the phone had been segregated due to battery condition.

### **Battery Escalation Timing Unknown**

No timeline establishes:

- when the battery issue was first identified,
- whether the issue existed before shipment prep,
- or whether the condition was contemporaneously documented.

## **E. Root Cause Adequacy**

The root cause is generally strong because it focuses on:

- custody controls,
- disposition authorization,
- and accountability failures.

However, the package could still be challenged for insufficient specificity regarding:

- who maintained release authority,
- whether Property Management controls existed but were bypassed,
- or whether no formal process existed at all.

The draft would become stronger if it clarified:

whether the failure was procedural noncompliance or absence of enforced controls.

## **F. Corrective Action Adequacy**

Corrective actions are generally credible and tied to the identified control failures.

Strong areas:

- verbal authorization prohibition,
- custody transfer requirements,
- battery escalation controls,
- role-based training.

Remaining weakness:  
The actions do not yet address:

- verification of Government property marking/tagging,
- escalation responsibility ownership,
- or periodic audits of shipment staging areas.

## **G. Supporting Documentation Gaps**

The following evidence gaps remain unresolved:

- No written PM instruction
- No customer authorization
- No PLCO direction
- No Contracting Officer authorization
- No battery incident documentation
- No EHS documentation
- No disposal manifest
- No ITAD/recycling documentation
- No custody transfer record
- No final disposition documentation

The absence of these records does not invalidate the package, but it significantly limits the ability to substantiate the disposal narrative.

## **H. Escalation / Audit Exposure Areas**

### **Potential Unauthorized Disposition**

Because no documented authorization chain exists, DCMA may evaluate this as:

- potential unauthorized disposition,
- release without Property Management approval,
- or breakdown of disposition authority controls.

### **Hazardous Material Handling Exposure**

The battery condition introduces possible:

- EHS involvement,
- hazardous waste handling requirements,
- and emergency processing questions.

The package currently acknowledges this appropriately without overstating compliance.

### **Custody Control Exposure**

The largest operational concern remains:

the inability to establish documented custody and movement control between shipment expectation and loss discovery.

That is likely to become the central DCMA review focus.

## **Overall Assessment**

The package is substantially stronger than a typical LTDD draft because it:

- preserves uncertainty,
- separates interview statements from verified facts,
- avoids unsupported disposal conclusions,
- and uses control-based causal analysis.

Primary remaining risks involve:

- incomplete custody reconstruction,
- unresolved storage/access details,
- lack of documented authorization chain,
- and wording that occasionally implies movement or disposition more strongly than the evidence supports.
