## **Biggest DCMA Risk Right Now**

The largest risk is turning the disposal story into a documented “fact” without supporting evidence.

Right now, you have:

- interview statements suggesting disposal,
- but no documented authorization,
- no disposal record,
- and no final disposition evidence.

That creates a major defensibility issue if the draft states the phone “was disposed.”  

## **High-Risk Rejection Areas**

### **Unsupported Disposal Language**

DCMA will likely challenge statements such as:

“The phone was disposed.”  
“The customer approved disposal.”  
“The PM authorized disposal.”

None of those are currently documented.

The safer position is:

Personnel stated the phone may have been disposed of; however, no documentation confirming authorized disposition has been identified.

### **Missing Authorization Chain**

Right now, there is:

- no written PM instruction,
- no customer authorization,
- no PLCO involvement,
- and no contracting authority evidence.

That creates risk around unauthorized disposition of Government property.

### **No Final Disposition Documentation**

You currently have:

- no disposal manifest,
- no recycling/ITAD record,
- no destruction certificate,
- no hazardous waste documentation,
- and no accountability closure trail.

DCMA will likely ask:

“How do you know what happened to the asset?”

Right now, the answer is:  
you do not know yet.

### **Weak Custody Trail**

There are still major unanswered questions:

- where the phone was stored,
- who controlled access,
- who last physically verified it,
- whether movement was documented,
- and whether it remained tagged as Government property.

Those gaps become major review findings in LTDD investigations.  

### **Hazardous Battery Handling Exposure**

George described a potentially hazardous battery condition, but:

- no EHS documentation exists,
- no battery incident record exists,
- and no emergency handling documentation exists.

If the draft references battery disposal or hazardous handling as fact, DCMA may expect supporting environmental or safety records.

## **Drafting Risks**

### **Unsafe Language**

Avoid:

“George disposed of the phone.”

Avoid:

“The battery issue required disposal.”

Avoid:

“The phone was properly handled.”

Those statements overstate the evidence.

### **Weak Root Cause Language**

Do not use:

“Miscommunication”  
“Human error”  
“People assumed disposal was approved”

DCMA typically expects control failures, not blame-based explanations.  

## **Current Safe Position**

Right now, the safest defensible position is:

The phone cannot currently be physically accounted for, and interviews suggest it may have been removed due to a reported battery condition; however, documentation confirming authorized disposition, customer approval, or final disposition has not been identified.

That preserves uncertainty while avoiding unsupported conclusions.
